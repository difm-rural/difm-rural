# Rural Connections v0.1 experience review

Reviewed 13 October 2026 against local main, commit 08de0e6.

## Assessment

The core structure is sound: Home, Jobs, Listings, Activity and Account. Keep these five destinations for v0.1. The biggest opportunity is to make labels, actions and results agree throughout the journey. Several current actions promise a destination or outcome they do not deliver. Fix those before spending time on additional features or a broad redesign.

This is a source-based review of navigation, screen content and handlers. No running-device walkthrough, visual rendering, live database validation or delivery tests were performed. Layout, scrolling, keyboard behaviour, permissions, screen-reader behaviour and actual email delivery remain device checks. GitHub currency could not be confirmed because the remote connection failed. No application code was changed during this review.

## Fix before inviting testers

### 1. Guest listing enquiries lead to an unavailable route

Evidence: `src/screens/ServiceDetailScreen.js:507` sends enquiry-only listings directly to `Chat`. The guest navigator in `src/navigation/AppNavigator.js:380` does not register Chat. Unlike booking requests, this action has no sign-in handoff.

Impact: a new visitor can browse grazing, lease or for-sale listings, then encounter an action that cannot complete.

Fix: offer sign-in at the moment of enquiry, preserve the listing and intended recipient, then open the conversation after sign-in and any onboarding. Explain: “Sign in to message the person who listed this.”

Acceptance: from a fresh install, open an enquiry-only listing, sign in, finish or skip setup, and arrive in that listing's conversation. Back returns to a sensible destination.

### 2. Onboarding action cards do not perform their labelled actions

Evidence: `src/screens/OnboardingScreen.js:550–570`: both “Post a task” and “Browse services” invoke `markComplete`, which only completes onboarding. `src/navigation/AppNavigator.js:613` subsequently shows the authenticated app without forwarding either intent.

Fix: use “Post a job” and “Browse listings”, and carry the selected destination through completion. Keep a separate “Explore the app” action for landing on Home. If completion fails, stay on the screen with a retry; `markComplete` currently ignores the update error.

Acceptance: each action opens its named destination, and a failed profile update does not falsely finish setup.

### 3. Booking language promises confirmation too early

Evidence: `src/screens/BookingConfirmScreen.js:273,430` uses “Confirm booking”; its payload is `status: 'pending'` and the result is “Booking requested”. The same final button is used for quote-required requests. The guest sheet also says “confirm your booking”.

Fix: use “Review your request” as the heading; “Send booking request” for priced services and “Request a quote” for quote-required services. Explain the next step before submission: “The provider will review your request. Your booking is confirmed when they accept.” For quotes: “The provider will send a quote for you to review.” Avoid promising they will respond “shortly”.

Acceptance: quote and priced requests have appropriate wording, and pending requests are never presented as confirmed.

### 4. Price totals imply more certainty than the calculation supports

Evidence: `src/screens/ServiceDetailScreen.js:115` calculates rate × quantity without minimum charge; `src/screens/BookingConfirmScreen.js:92` does the same. Listing details display minimum charges, terms and extra charges, but the request summary labels the result “Total”. Variant minimum charges are also displayed without participating in that calculation.

Fix: decide whether minimum charge is enforced in the estimate; apply the same rule to detail, review and stored request amounts. If extras remain display-only, show “Base estimate” with “Extra charges may apply — agree the final price with the provider”, and repeat relevant extras and terms in the request review. Keep direct payment wording visible. Do not call an incomplete calculation a final total.

Acceptance example: a $50/hour listing with a $150 minimum cannot imply that one hour costs $50 all-in. A listing with delivery charges clearly explains whether delivery is included.

### 5. “View booking” does not open a booking

Evidence: `src/screens/ServiceDetailScreen.js:522–526`: this action opens an alert hardcoded to “Pending confirmation”, rather than opening the booking record.

Fix: navigate to `ServiceBookingDetail` with the actual booking and requester role. Render the current state rather than a fixed pending message.

Acceptance: pending, confirmed and in-progress bookings each open the correct detail and show their actual status.

### 6. Sign-in needs a finished, reliable first impression

Evidence: `src/screens/LoginScreen.js:325` says “Rural Connect”, while the rest of the product says Rural Connections. Lines 373–379 show a disabled “Continue with Google” button. `resendCode` at line 214 ignores errors and always announces “Code sent”.

Fix: use the complete product name, remove the unavailable Google option for this release, and handle resend failures honestly. Add a resend busy state and cooldown, helpful email validation, and guard against repeated verification submissions. Make email-code paste/autofill easy; the present six-box entry supports paste, but needs device verification. Use human-readable failures with a clear next action.

Acceptance: invalid email, failed resend, expired code and successful pasted code all behave predictably. No unavailable login method is offered.

### 7. Search and network failure must not look like an empty marketplace

Evidence: `src/screens/LandingScreen.js:65–108` ignores fetch errors and falls back to empty arrays; its empty state says “Nothing listed yet” even when a search finds no matches. `src/screens/tabs/BrowseTabScreen.js:110` similarly ignores listing fetch errors, and filtered zero results show “No listings yet” with “Add a listing”. Both screens place pull-to-refresh inside the nonempty content branch.

Fix: distinguish loading, request failure, genuinely empty results and no search matches. Supply “Retry” for errors and “Clear search” for no matches. Keep refresh accessible when empty. Preserve the query when retrying.

Suggested copy: “Couldn't load listings. Check your connection and try again.” / “No listings match ‘tractor’. Try another word or clear your search.”

Acceptance: no connection, an empty database and an unmatched search each show a different useful state.

## Improve organisation before release

### 8. Complete the vocabulary migration

Evidence: guest navigation says “Services” (`LandingScreen.js:210`) and “Available tasks” (line 180); onboarding still says “Post a task” and “Browse services”; Home's empty summary also mentions browsing services. The authenticated tabs already say Jobs and Listings.

Use one vocabulary:

| Concept | Wording |
| --- | --- |
| Work someone needs done | Job |
| Response to a posted job | Offer |
| Advertised service, grazing, hire, lease or item | Listing |
| Message about a listing | Enquiry |
| Unaccepted service request | Booking request |
| Accepted service arrangement | Booking |

“Service” remains useful when describing the service kind of listing. Avoid renaming technical identifiers purely for this copy pass.

### 9. Listings should prioritise discovery

Evidence: `src/lib/roles.js` makes `canProvide` universally true. `BrowseTabScreen.js:289` therefore displays “Your listings” and “Other listings” for signed-in users; the “Near you” discovery branch is bypassed. The query also merges paused and closed own listings into that discovery screen.

Recommendation: make Listings primarily a place to discover live listings. Keep “My listings” as one secondary action that opens management, where live, paused and closed states belong. Use brief descriptive text such as “Services, grazing, hire and local goods”. Preserve simple search for v0.1; avoid reinstating a large filter toolbar without user evidence.

Acceptance: a first-time user without listings gets a useful discovery page. A user with paused or closed listings can manage them without mixing them into public discovery.

### 10. Activity should explain active work and make messages retrievable

Evidence: the “In progress” segment includes open jobs and pending requests (`ActivityTabScreen.js:655`). Its empty-state “Post a job” action navigates to Home instead of the posting flow (lines 681–689). Home's “Open jobs” tile counts all active job statuses (`HomeTabScreen.js:110,179`).

Fix: call the segment “Active”; name summary tiles for what they actually count. Send “Post a job” straight to the wizard. Keep Jobs focused on discovery and posting, and Activity as the reliable place to track one's own arrangements.

Enquiry conversations deserve a dedicated retrieval check: Activity is organised around jobs, offers and bookings, while Connections covers completed work (`src/lib/connections.js`). Notifications provide a route back to conversations, but the inspected primary screens do not offer a general listing-enquiry inbox. Ensure someone who sends a message can find that conversation after leaving it, even before receiving a reply. A compact Messages entry within Activity is preferable to another main tab.

### 11. Onboarding can be lighter and clearer

Evidence: four views precede Home; profile fields include full name, display name, phone, location and bio. “Offer work?” describes selecting skills, which can sound like offering employment. “Skip setup” completes the whole flow rather than skipping just the optional stage. The welcome copy says three steps while the progress UI has four positions.

Recommendation: collect a clear public name and location first; mark optional fields explicitly and defer qualifications and notification tuning. Use “What can you help with?” for skills. Distinguish “Skip for now” for an optional step from “Finish setup later” for leaving the flow. Consider two essential setup steps with an optional skills section after them. Keep a short Jobs/Listings explanation on the first screen.

Verify public-name behaviour before promising “This is how you'll appear to others”: many cards and provider details currently display `full_name`, whereas onboarding calls `display_name` the public name. The welcome claim about contact details should also be checked against actual database access rules before retaining it.

### 12. Put help and feedback where testers will look

Evidence: “Contact us” and “Help & FAQ” live inside Account → Privacy (`AccountTabScreen.js:931–958`). How it works says to contact the team from Account without naming the additional step. “Help & FAQ” opens an explainer with no FAQ section.

Fix: add clearly labelled “Help & feedback” at the Account hub. Use “How it works” for the existing explainer, and “Send feedback” for the feedback channel. Make the install/version number easy to include in a report. Match labels to destinations.

Acceptance: a tester can find help and submit feedback without being told where to look. Confirm the email link works on a phone without a configured mail client, or provide a copyable address as fallback.

## Recommended screen responsibilities

| Destination | Primary purpose | Recommended contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home | Decide what to do next | Post a job, find jobs, browse listings, create a listing; genuinely actionable items below |
| Jobs | Find or post work | Open jobs, search/sort/distance, obvious Post a job action; restrained shortcuts to own work |
| Listings | Discover advertised things | Live listings, simple search, descriptive kind labels; secondary My listings action |
| Activity | Follow existing arrangements | Active, History, Messages and notifications; one clear next action per item |
| Account | Identity and preferences | Profile, My listings, alerts/preferences, Help & feedback, privacy/security |

Guest navigation should use the same nouns and explain the same Jobs/Listings split. The current Home/Listings/Post/Account guest structure can remain, provided Post is explicitly “Post a job” and all gated actions preserve intent.

## Visual and device release checks

Keep the forest green brand, shared buttons and existing card system. Source inspection cannot establish whether they feel polished on a phone. Check these with the actual release build:

- Small phone and enlarged text: headings, long listing titles, bottom buttons and tab labels remain readable. Shared Button currently restricts labels to one line; test long actions rather than assuming they fit.
- Keyboard: email code, description, prices and chat remain visible and reachable; Back dismisses or navigates predictably without losing entered work.
- Permissions: location declined, photos declined and notifications declined all leave usable routes forward.
- Forms: retain values when moving back, explain disabled Next buttons, and prevent accidental duplicate submissions.
- Interrupted work: test app backgrounding and termination during a job/listing draft. Job context is memory-backed, and guest storage happens at submission; do not promise general draft persistence without implementing it.
- Headers: consistent brand name, back labels, spacing and title sizes; intentionally distinguish workflow headers without making each flow feel like a different product.
- Empty content: seed enough realistic jobs and listing kinds to exercise the app, and also test the truly empty experience.
- Accessibility: check text contrast, touch targets, enlarged text and screen-reader labels on important controls. Existing accessibility labels and shared tokens are a useful foundation.

## Friends-and-family walkthrough

Give each tester tasks, not tap-by-tap instructions. Observe where they hesitate. Ask them what they expect before pressing important actions.

1. With a fresh installation, explain what Jobs and Listings mean after looking at the first screen.
2. Find a listing and contact its owner, starting while signed out.
3. Create an account with an email code; complete or skip profile setup.
4. Post a fence-repair job with a location, photo and flexible budget. Change something on the review screen before posting.
5. With another test account, find that job and send an offer. Accept it as the job owner, message each other and complete the work.
6. Request a priced service and then a quote-required service. Explain whether either is confirmed and what happens next.
7. Create one service listing and one enquiry-only listing, then edit or pause one.
8. Send a listing enquiry, leave the screen and find the conversation again before receiving a reply.
9. Search for something absent, deny location access, and briefly go offline. Recover without restarting.
10. Find help and send feedback identifying the screen and what happened.

Record: device/build, task, expected result, actual result, hesitation point, completion without assistance, and any lost work. Test notifications and email with real delivery separately; the source review cannot confirm them.

## Release order

1. Resolve unavailable enquiry routes and incorrect action destinations.
2. Align booking language, real status and price expectations.
3. Finish sign-in, error recovery and empty/search states.
4. Apply the vocabulary and organisation pass, including accessible feedback and conversation retrieval.
5. Run the device walkthrough, fix observed layout/keyboard problems, then invite the wider tester group.

For this release, a predictable journey with clear words and working recovery will contribute more to a professional first impression than additional features.
