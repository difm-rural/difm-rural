# Connections and Trusted Locals discovery

**18 November 2026 · Product proposal for review**

Rural Connections already has a useful repeat-work model. A completed job or booking makes the pair visible in their work history. A job invitation asks a previous provider to consider a specific job. A saved provider would solve a third problem: remembering someone before or between engagements. Present these together under **My Locals**, with separate meanings and permissions.

## Evidence and scope

Reviewed the Expo navigation, provider-web screens and server actions, repository schema and migrations, and notification functions. A read-only hosted catalog check confirmed participant filtering in `connections`, the invitation INSERT grant and policy, status-only UPDATE access, private saved-interest ownership policy, and several privacy boundaries below. No live marketplace rows were read or changed. The 27 targeted repository tests passed through the configured loader. They cover grouping across roles, shared-history scoping, safe profile projection, repeat-job draft handling, mocked invitation actions, and navigation. They do not establish live delivery or end-to-end mobile parity. Prior hosted synthetic smoke evidence confirms an invited job could progress through offer, award and completion, with unrelated viewers denied private photos. ## Data model and permissions

- **Completed work.** `public.connections` is a derived view grouped by directional requester/provider pair. Sources are completed jobs joined to accepted bids and completed bookings. Both participants can see their relationships through the view's explicit `auth.uid()` predicate. Nobody sends or accepts a Connection. Completion automatically contributes while source records qualify. Engagement dates are posting or booking creation dates. Removing a qualifying record can change history; there is no relationship row to delete, unfriend or block. A single account can be requester and provider, with both directions coming from the same data. [Connections view](../supabase/migrations/20260630120000_connections_view.sql)
- **Private saves.** `saved_interests` has a `provider` kind, a provider ID, delivery settings and owner-only RLS. The job alert matcher has no provider-ID predicate. A provider-kind record therefore is not evidence that provider-specific alerts work. Existing watchlists track jobs, and saved searches track criteria. Neither represents completed work. [Saved interests and matcher](../supabase/migrations/20260726130000_saved_interests.sql)
- **Invitations.** `job_invites` stores job, requester, provider, creation time and `pending`, `seen` or `declined`. Its unique job/provider pair prevents duplicate rows, and the job foreign key cascades on deletion. Both named parties can read their invitation. The recipient can directly update only `status`, with no transition restriction identified in that grant and policy. There is no supported revoke, re-invite after decline, or user-block action. Web's `invite_connection_to_job` verifies the signed-in job owner, open status, an eligible prior provider in that direction and an existing public profile. `create_invited_job` creates the job and invite together. The photo publisher enforces equivalent eligibility explicitly. [Invitation schema](../supabase/migrations/20260630130000_job_invites.sql) · [Column grant](../supabase/migrations/20260703120000_security_fix_pack.sql) · [Web invitation RPCs](../supabase/migrations/20261019205939_web_connection_invites.sql) · [Photo publisher](../supabase/migrations/20261117120000_job_primary_media.sql)
- **Visibility and profile data.** An invite can grant job access. Completed work and saving a person confer no general access to their jobs or personal details. Profiles use `profiles_public` for cross-user reads, excluding phone, address and coordinates. Job-facing views and private photo serving have their own audience checks. Reviews are readable through the existing public review policy. The UI prompts for reviews after completion. [Profile projection](../supabase/migrations/20260629130000_profiles_pii_lockdown.sql) · [Job audience helper](../supabase/migrations/20260630140000_fix_policy_recursion.sql) · [Media audience](../supabase/migrations/20261118120000_job_photo_editing.sql) · [Review policies](../supabase/migrations/00000000000000_baseline.sql)
- **Delivery.** An invite INSERT triggers `new_job_invite` for the named provider. Clients handle job metadata through Notifications. Push uses the existing notification webhook and Edge Function. Email eligibility uses the outbox and its processor. No relationship Edge Function or separate notification system is needed. [Invite trigger](../supabase/migrations/20260630130000_job_invites.sql) · [Push webhook](../supabase/migrations/20261007120000_push_webhook_vault.sql) · [Email outbox processor](../supabase/functions/process-email-outbox/index.ts) · [Web notification action](../provider-web/src/app/notifications/actions.ts)

## Inconsistencies and gaps to resolve

1. **Invitation eligibility is not uniformly enforced.** The hosted direct INSERT policy checks that the signed-in member owns the job and supplies their own requester ID. It lacks the prior-provider and open-job restrictions found in the RPC. Mobile uses this wider path for existing jobs and ordinary invited creation. An invite can grant access and generate a notification, so move supported clients to the same guarded operation before removing the direct grant or tightening it. Test older Expo builds during that transition.
2. **Decline and access mean different things.** Both inboxes hide declined records while the job audience helper and private-media predicate check invitation existence. An invited person may still have access through a direct route after declining. Expo marks seen when it opens job detail. Web's invitation action marks seen from the inbox. Notification-origin entry and retry should follow one rule. Decide explicitly whether decline keeps access and how a prior offer or award affects it before changing either surface.
3. **Masked web reads and mobile base-table reads diverge.** Web invitation cards read `jobs_public`. The mobile inbox embeds `job:job_id(*)`. Hosted catalog confirms the authenticated role has base `jobs.latitude` SELECT and the invite helper ignores status. This creates a potential pre-award exact-location path where row access is granted. Confirm with synthetic participant fixtures and use the same masked projection on both clients. The separately documented `job_owner_id(uuid)` disclosure remains outstanding; the hosted catalog still grants guest EXECUTE. [Mobile invitation loader](../src/lib/invites.js) · [Existing privacy finding](marketplace-health-production-readiness.md)
4. **History and reputation can be misread.** Mobile hides work supplied to past requesters from Connections. Web merges both directions, while the first and last dates describe when the work originated. Use role labels and **Work history** wording. Keep profile rating counts and role-specific review lists consistent. Never imply that completion or a private save means an endorsement.
5. **Work history and discovery are separate journeys today.** A member can find someone via an offer or listing and still have no way to save their profile. A recipient of a shared signed-in profile URL may meet a login wall. Decide the destination before promoting profile sharing.

## Proposed unified experience

| User-facing term | Meaning and interaction |
|---|---|
| **My Locals** | One destination on Home on both clients, using the existing Connections routes. Show **Saved** and **Worked together** in one list or two simple filters. Deduplicate by member and show both markers when applicable. Keep incoming **Job invitations** easy to reach from the Jobs area and Notifications. |
| **Saved** | Private, one-sided bookmark. Provide **Save provider** and **Remove from saved** on the provider profile. Saving sends no notification, confers no permissions and does not imply previous work. |
| **Worked together** | Derived history visible to both participants. Show **Helped you** and **You helped** where relevant. Provide links to completed work, profile, reviews and current listings. |
| **Invite to a job** | Specific request for an offer, with explicit audience and delivery confirmation. Surface it on a past provider's profile and shared history when they previously supplied work to the current requester. Keep saved-only providers browseable and bookable while invitation eligibility remains restricted. |
| **Share profile** | Device share or copy link to a privacy-safe provider destination. Use existing work-based reviews as reputation evidence. A neighbour sees the provider's public information, without either person's private list or work history. |
| **Repeat this job** / **View current listings** | Existing repeat and booking entry points. A repeat is a new draft with fresh timing; a current listing opens the normal booking or enquiry flow. |

**Requester journey.** Find a provider from a listing or offer, save them privately, and return to My Locals to browse their current work. After completed work, the same entry also shows shared history. Invite an eligible previous provider to a new or open job, with an explicit audience choice for new jobs. To recommend someone, use **Share profile** once the recipient landing is defined.

**Provider journey.** Open My Locals to see past requesters, even if the member has never hired someone. Receive and act on invitations through Jobs and Notifications. Keep a useful profile and active listings, with ordinary reviews supporting reputation. Permanent or paid endorsements, a feed and connection requests add little to this workflow.

## Implementation stages and decisions

1. **Unify existing history first.** Reuse the `connections` view, web bidirectional grouping, Expo connection rows/avatar, and existing profile and Activity routes. Give both platforms the same Home entry, labels, and an explicit unavailable state on load failure. Ship with existing invite actions and no schema change in this stage.
2. **Align invitations before expanding them.** Reuse the guarded RPCs, notification trigger, accepted-offer flow and privacy-safe projections. Cover non-owner, stranger, closed job and retry cases, preserve supported old-client behavior during rollout, and verify decline and location access with local fixtures. This may require a targeted grant, policy or RPC migration after review. It needs no new invitation entity.
3. **Add a private save using the existing provider-kind record if it can remain inert.** Use `frequency='off'` and no push or email, keep owner-only reads/writes, and enforce one bookmark per member/provider pair without disturbing existing alert rows. Check for existing provider-kind data or alert semantics before a unique constraint or migration. Keep this record out of alert matching and generic alert toggles. A small dedicated owner/provider bookmark table is a fallback only if the legacy provider-kind record cannot safely represent a bookmark.
4. **Add sharing when the destination is clear.** Reuse native share and the web share/copy fallback. Share only the safe provider projection or an existing public listing until a guest-safe profile route is agreed. Defer provider-follow alerts, recommendation scores, referrals, social graphs, recurring work automation and relationship removal. Handle blocking separately as a safety policy spanning invitations and messaging if product requires it.

**Product decisions**

- Choose **My Locals** or retain **Connections** as the navigation label during rollout. Decide whether **Saved** and **Worked together** appear as filters or sections.
- Keep invitations limited to people who previously provided completed work for the requester, or eventually let requesters invite saved providers. Wider eligibility needs controls for unsolicited invitations.
- Define decline, revoke and re-invite behavior, including continuing access for an existing winner and whether a requester should receive a decline alert.
- Choose what a neighbour can see before signing in, and whether provider sharing needs an opt-out.
- Decide how missing/deleted work should affect visible history. Use engagement dates unless a dependable completion-date source is agreed.
